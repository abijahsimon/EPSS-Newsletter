Xiaoyan ZhouBALBOA: photographing the aurora in broad daylight +

Since Martin Brendel took the first auroral photograph in January 1892, auroras have only ever been imaged in darkness — the sunlit atmosphere is simply too bright, from the ground or from orbit. BALLoon-Based Observations for Sunlit Aurora, funded by NASA's LCAS program in 2018, is the pathfinder that gets around the problem by flying at about 40 km, above 99.7% of the atmosphere.

The team's auroral experiments with an InGaAs near-infrared camera recorded the first Meinel (0–0) band aurora during twilight, when the sky is about as bright as it is at float altitude — confirming the approach works. Four test flights out of Fort Sumner, New Mexico followed, along with fourteen field validations, three ground campaigns at Poker Flat, and a 2023 piggyback flight that caught gravity waves riding the O₂ dayglow.

The primary auroral flight is planned for 2027. Five UCLA students across Computer Science, Physics & Astronomy, and Mechanical & Aerospace Engineering, plus a postdoc from Arizona, built and tested the payload.

Peng NiExploring planetary history with the Sapphire Lab +

Peng Ni's research moves between the Moon, meteorites, diamonds, and high-pressure experiments that recreate planetary interiors.

The Sapphire Lab's Nu Sapphire mass spectrometer reads isotope fingerprints in elements including potassium, iron, copper, and magnesium, while field courses take students onto desert dry lakebeds to search for meteorites.

Award Highlight

Amy Mainzer wins the Robert Holland Jr. Award

The Research Corporation for Science Advancement created the Holland Award to honor senior scientists for research, teaching, mentorship, and their impact on helping students reach their full potential. Mainzer is one of three recipients in 2026, recognized for her asteroid and comet research and for her public engagement with science.

She leads NASA's Near-Earth Object Surveyor mission, a space telescope under construction to find the majority of potentially hazardous asteroids and comets, and hosts the podcast Unobtanium.

Award Highlight

Jean-Luc Margot elected to the Royal Academy of Belgium

The Academy traces its roots to the Imperial and Royal Academy of Science and Letters of Brussels, established in 1772. Membership in the Classe des Sciences is limited to 60 members and 50 associate members — foreign members or Belgians residing abroad.

Past members include André-Marie Ampère, Charles Darwin, Georges Lemaître, Harold Urey, and Nobel laureates Christian de Duve, Ilya Prigogine, and François Englert.
