Dispatches from the Field

Spring 2026 field dispatches from EPSS courses across California, Nevada, Utah, and the desert Southwest.

By John He

On behalf of the EPSS field committee · Photo essay

Dear friends, colleagues, and the EPSS community,

Welcome to “Dispatches from the Field.” As we make our way through Spring Quarter and yet another year of teaching, mentoring, and learning in the field, we want to celebrate the breadth of field experiences happening across EPSS. From local day trips to multi-day excursions across California, Nevada, Utah, and beyond, undergraduate and graduate students continue to encounter our planet directly: in landscapes, outcrops, field notes, conversations, and the shared problem-solving that happens best outside the classroom.

Field learning remains one of the most powerful things we do as a department. There is no substitute for standing in front of an exposure of solid Earth, tracing a geologic contact with your own eyes, working through an interpretation with classmates, and seeing how quickly a landscape can turn an abstract concept into something tangible.

The scale of field learning in EPSS is special.

It reflects an extraordinary amount of planning, care, expertise, and commitment from faculty and staff, the support of alumni and friends of EPSS, and the curiosity of students willing to start early, stay dusty, ask careful questions, and learn from the ground beneath them.

This spring’s field moments included Historical and Regional Geology at Valley of Fire and Zion, Structural Geology at Painted Canyon, Igneous Petrology at the Bishop Tuff and White Mountain Research Station, and Stratigraphic and Field Geology with Mackenzie Day.

Keeping this tradition strong is something we do together, and we hope to continue sharing these adventures with you in future dispatches.

The complete field photo archive is held in UCLA Box. Access may require a UCLA Box sign-in.
