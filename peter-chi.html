Farewell · In his own words

On leaving, and on raising a member of Generation Alpha

Peter Chi

Researcher, Earth, Planetary, and Space Sciences · June 2026

With my wife’s unwavering support, I decided a few years ago to gradually transition from a standard academic schedule to one that would allow me more time to raise our son during his critical early years.

I have always been fascinated by how human beings — the most intelligent life form on Earth — begin to learn and create. Witnessing my child’s cognitive growth up close has given me a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity to see that creative spark firsthand, while fully embracing my role as a parent.

It was by no means an easy decision; UCLA has been my spiritual home ever since I first arrived in the United States for graduate school. Over the years, my wife and I frequently had to navigate our own “two-body problem.” Yet I remained steadfastly anchored at UCLA, willingly embracing long commutes — initially from Orange County, later from the Bay Area, and eventually all the way from Washington, D.C. — before we happily resettled in Southern California in 2021.

Looking back on my years at UCLA, I feel incredibly fortunate to have been a part of Professor Chris Russell’s research group. I had the rare privilege of observing firsthand how this most prolific space scientist built a publication record that remains unparalleled, even in this modern age of AI-assisted research. I also feel deeply blessed to have learned from and collaborated with the world-class scientists who define the UCLA space science community. It was a genuine joy to mentor so many talented students who have since gone on to thrive in their own careers.

I must also take this opportunity to extend a special thanks to the EPSS administrative staff. Your truly heroic efforts to save my NASA and NSF proposals in the final hours before midnight deadlines remain some of the most exhilarating and memorable moments of my professional life!

While still in its early stages, my journey of educating a member of “Generation Alpha” has already transformed my mind. The landscape in which young minds learn science today is vastly different from the past, and the world they inherit faces an entirely new set of challenges. Nonetheless, it is deeply reassuring to see that curiosity and a passion for discovery are fundamental to human nature, igniting in early childhood.

Looking ahead, I see my ongoing mission as one of continued service to the space science community — whether as a journal editor, an educator for the next generation, or in whatever capacity the future may call for. In the meantime, consider this an open invitation to review papers for Geophysical Research Letters and Earth, Planets and Space! I look forward to crossing paths with many of you at future events.

Peter Chi
